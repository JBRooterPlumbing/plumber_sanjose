Decision Points and Variations

Symptom or Confirmed Leak?

A water stain, damp area, sound, bill increase, or customer concern can justify investigation without proving a plumbing leak.

Active Leak or Historical Moisture Evidence?

Staining can remain after a previous moisture event. Do not describe every stain as evidence of a currently active leak.

Plumbing Leak or Another Moisture Source?

Moisture may have plumbing or non-plumbing causes. Roof intrusion, exterior water intrusion, drainage conditions, condensation, irrigation, or another source may require separate evaluation.

Indoor or Outdoor?

Meter and shutoff observations may help narrow the general context in appropriate situations, but they do not automatically identify the exact failed component.

Visible or Concealed?

A visible leak has directly observable evidence near the relevant escape point. A concealed leak has a source or path that is not readily visible. These require different levels of documentation and diagnostic certainty.

Leak Location or Leak Source?

Location describes where evidence places the leak. Source identifies what is actually allowing water to escape. Keep the two terms separate.

Detection or Repair?

Detection identifies or narrows a problem. Repair addresses a supported defect. Neither should automatically be used as a substitute for the other.

Water-Bill Indicator or Proof?

A higher bill can prompt investigation, but water consumption can change for multiple reasons. A bill increase alone is not definitive proof of a leak.
